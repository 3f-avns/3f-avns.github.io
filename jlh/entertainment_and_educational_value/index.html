Entertainment and Educational Value

Educational Value

Historical Accuracy

While the plot of Hero is a fantasy, details of the movie are still historically accurate. Qin Shi Huangdi was the first emperor of China, and did unify the warring states. He was also extremely cruel, and burned books. Because of his cruelty, he gained many enemies, who would try to assassinate him. Hence the plot.

According to an article from Wordpress, Qin Shi Huangdi's reputation has been being rehabilitated in the modern time period. This film shares in the effort to rehabilitate Qin Shi Huangdi's reputation, by painting him as a benevolent monarch, who wishes for the good of all people in China. There may be some political meaning behind this. Please refer to the section on Contemporary Meaning if you wish to learn more.

Another interesting detail of Hero is that of the name of Nameless. According to an article, the Qin state was comparable to Sparta in how they functioned. Young members of society would grow up in a group, which they would accompany into adulthood, and fight alongside. This also meant that everyone was known in the Qin state, so having a Nameless hero would be very unlikely. The article also mentions how punishment for all was customary. This means that if Nameless was given a name, and failed to assassinate the king, his whole extended family would be killed also. Having Nameless be called by the alias Nameless is a deliberate choice to foreshadow how Nameless will lie to the king, and has a plot to assassinate him.

Contemporary Meaning

In the final act of Hero, after Nameless' lie, and the truth, have been revealed, Broken Sword pleads with Nameless to not kill the king. He uses his sword to draw the letters 天下 in the sand. These two symbols represent tianxia, or All Under Heaven. Tianxia's meaning in China has historically been that every man, woman, and child should follow civilized behavior (according to this article). Broken Sword draws this symbol to try to convince Nameless that Qin Shi Huangdi, despite being a warlord, will bring about good for all people. When they are “All Under Heaven”.

Hero was released in 2002, less than a year after the terrorist attacks on 9/11. MIRAMAX, the studio that translated Hero into English for international viewing, translated tianxia as “Our Land”. The translation of tianxia as “Our Land” gives more contemporary meaning to the film. “Our Land” can be interpreted as a cry for peace, in the post 9/11 world. This translation is still relevant today, because world-peace still does not exist. The message that I take from “Our Land” is that everyone deserves a place in our land, and that no one should be discriminated against.

Hero was also made under Chinese Communist rule. There are clear connections that can be made to the political agenda of the Chinese Communist party, and the film. The idea of unifying the Chinese states, for the good of all people, is one of these connections. Another can be made during the final minutes of the film. Nameless sacrifices himself for the good of all, which is a very political message. The people of China should sacrifice themselves so that China can exist, and be good for all people. Hero promotes the idea that authoritarian control is better than democratic chaos.

According to an article from the Asian Educational Media Service, some critics of the film say that the political messaging could even support the conquest and incorporation of Taiwan. Qin Shi Huangdi is also portrayed differently in Hero. According to the article, “For centuries Confucians castigated Qin Shi Huang as a tyrant who buried scholars and burned books”, implying that Hero chose to give Qin Shi Huangdi a more positive cultural view. This may be for political reasons.

Entertainment Value

Hero is a very entertaining film. Director Yimou Zhang uses color, imagery, and special effects to make Hero as entertaining as it is. Just about every second of it is stylized, leading to quite an entertaining movie.

Color is used to distinguish and give more meaning to different sections of the movie. Most sections of the movie, such as the fight scene between Sky and Nameless, feature a strongly present color, which represents different emotions. For example, the first re-telling of how Broken Sword and Flying Snow died had an intense red theme. This highlights the heightened emotions that the re-telling centered around. Another example is when Broken Sword and Flying Snow storm the imperial palace. When Broken Sword enters into the inner palace, the main hall is decorated with thin green banners. The color of green is very strongly present in this scene, and represents the naivete of Qin Shi Huangdi. The green color persists into the whole section of the movie, which goes into Broken Sword and Flying Snow’s past. This is most likely done to show how naive they were, in their thinking. In the inner palace, Qin Shi Huangdi cuts down all of the green banners, visually showing how he learned from his mistakes, and is no longer as naive. Broken Sword and Flying Snow also change into White robes later on, to signal less naivete.

When Nameless tells Qin Shi Huangdi about how he killed Broken Sword and Flying Snow, there is a very prominent red theme, which highlights the emotions that Nameless supposedly exploits, to kill Broken Sword. After Qin Shi Huangdi deduced that Nameless was lying about some details, he tells a version of events that has Broken Sword and Flying Snow as less emotional. The scene is blue, which portrays the sequence of events as more somber than before. This is shown in detail when Broken Sword and Flying Snow have an emotional exchange where one of them must effectively die.

In addition to this color work, Hero also has high rating scores on sites like Rotten Tomatoes (rottentomatoes.com), where hero has a tomatometer rating of 94%, and a popcornmeter of 87%. The tomatometer has 193 fresh tomatoes, with only 12 rotten tomatoes. Top critics give Hero 51 fresh tomatoes, and no rotten tomatoes. One review goes as far as to say Hero is “A tremendously entertaining and visually rich spectacle.”
